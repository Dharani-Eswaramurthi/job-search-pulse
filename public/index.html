READ THE NUMBERS WELL

A pulse check.
A shared perspective.

This is an independent community survey created by an AI Engineer who is looking for a job change.

01

Experiences, not population statistics

People choose whether to take part. Respondents recruited through Reddit and other communities do not represent every job seeker. Results cannot establish national unemployment rates or prove why someone received an offer.

02

Consistent reporting windows

Most questions refer to the past 30 days. Reply and next-step questions refer to applications submitted 14–30 days before each person answered, giving employers some time to respond. A simple “application received” acknowledgment is not counted as a decision or next step.

03

What “communication stopped” means

An employer invited the applicant to proceed or spoke with them, then missed an agreed update date by at least 7 days—or gave no update for at least 14 days when no date was agreed. Explicit rejections and explained delays do not count.

04

Every chart has its own denominator

Percentages describe respondents, not applications. Missing, uncertain and not-applicable answers are excluded from that chart’s eligible responses. Channel and work-arrangement questions allow multiple selections, so their percentages may exceed 100% in total. Hiring stages show the furthest stage reported, not a conversion funnel.

05

Small groups stay out of the spotlight

Small cohorts and response categories are withheld. An additional category may be withheld so that a small count cannot be calculated from a chart total.

Only fixed-choice summaries are public. Names, emails, detailed location fields and written feedback are not displayed. Suppression reduces disclosure risk; overlapping groups and changing totals do not provide a guarantee of anonymity.

06

Fresh responses, with limits

The page checks for new summaries while it is open. Live updates normally appear within a few minutes, depending on caching and the data source. The weekly chart counts submissions; it does not measure how the job market has changed. Please submit once per survey round. Responses are self-reported and are not identity-verified.
